How Patients Choose a Dentist in 2026: What Changed at Every Step, From Search to Booking

AI answers now arrive before the first click, review recency counts as much as review totals, and online booking sits next to the phone. Here is the 2026 patient journey with sourced numbers, my own client data, and a 12 point audit you can run this week.

By Max Lezginov11 min readSix steps, any one can lose the patientEvery survey disclosed, vendors includedPublished 20 October 2026

Use of ChatGPT and other generative AI tools for local business recommendations rose from 6% to 45% of US consumers in one year, according to BrightLocal's Local Consumer Review Survey 2026, published in February 2026. BrightLocal sells review and local SEO software, surveyed 1,002 US adults through a SurveyMonkey panel, and covers all local businesses, not only dentists. For a practice, it means some patients meet your name, or a competitor's, inside an AI answer before they see your website.

The rest of the journey is changing more quietly. Patients want recent reviews, a higher star floor and a way to book without phoning. Below is each step with the latest data I could verify, plus what I see in my own client accounts.

Quick answer

Most patients in 2026 still start on Google and Maps, but more of them read an AI summary or ask a chatbot first. They shortlist practices by recent reviews and a 4 to 4.5 star floor, check the website for proof, insurance and prices, then contact you through whichever door is easiest: phone, form or online booking.

Key takeaways
  • 45% of US consumers used ChatGPT or other generative AI for local recommendations, up from 6% a year earlier (BrightLocal, 2026).
  • Google users clicked a regular result on 8% of visits with an AI summary, against 15% without (Pew Research Center, March 2025 data).
  • 74% of consumers look for reviews written in the last three months (BrightLocal, 2026).
  • 31% will only use a business rated 4.5 stars or higher, up from 17% a year earlier (BrightLocal, 2026).
  • At one Maryland practice, 360 of 821 tracked inquiries came through online booking, a channel that did not exist before (the practice's own GA4, 15 months).
How to read the numbers in this article

Two main sources are vendor surveys, and I label them each time. BrightLocal publishes no fieldwork dates. Tebra sells practice management, booking and review software; its 6th annual Patient Perspectives survey reached 3,964 US adults online on June 29, 2025, and asked about healthcare providers in general.

Pew Research Center, KFF, Gallup and the CDC are independent. For practice economics and visit rates, see my roundup of dental practice statistics for 2026.

What does the patient journey look like in 2026?

It still has six steps, the same six it had ten years ago. What changed is how much now happens before a patient reaches your website, and how many places there are to drop out.

What makes a patient start looking for a new dentist?

Something changes: a move, a new insurance plan, a toothache, or a wedding photo they did not love. Before searching for a dentist, many people search the problem itself, and more of them now ask an AI tool.

The CDC's FastStats page on oral and dental health, based on early release data from the 2025 National Health Interview Survey, puts the share of adults with a dental exam or cleaning in the past year at 66.7%. For the third of adults outside a recall routine, the next visit often starts with a trigger, not a reminder card.

In KFF's Tracking Poll on Health Information and Trust (1,343 US adults, February 24 to March 2, 2026, margin of error plus or minus 3 points), 32% of adults had used AI chatbots for health information in the past year. Of those users, 41% did it to look up information before deciding whether to see a provider at all.

The West Health-Gallup survey published in April 2026 (5,660 US adults, October 27 to December 22, 2025, plus or minus 2.1 points) found that one in four adults had used AI for health information or advice. Among recent users, 59% research on their own before visiting a doctor, and 14% said AI advice led them to skip a visit.

Age is the biggest divider. Pew Research Center's Americans and AI 2026 survey (5,119 US adults, February 17 to 23, 2026) found that 49% of adults ever use AI chatbots such as ChatGPT, Gemini or Copilot. 27% of adults under 30 said they use them to get medical advice, against 11% of adults 65 and older.

SourcePew Research Center, Americans and AI 2026: how opinions and use of AI differ by age · www.pewresearch.org/internet/2026/06/17/how-opinions-and-u…

Gallup saw the same slope: 69% of recent AI health users aged 18 to 29 research before seeing a doctor, against 43% of those 65 and older.

What this means for your practice: the first touchpoint is often a question, not a practice name. Pages that answer pre-treatment questions (how long, how much, does it hurt, will insurance cover it) let you enter the journey at step one. Where do patients search for a dentist now?

Google is still the starting point, but it no longer gets the first word. In Tebra's survey, 56% of patients said they start their research for a provider on Google, while 41% check practice websites and 38% use WebMD before booking.

BrightLocal's 2026 data shows how much the sources of local recommendations moved in a single year.

SourceBrightLocal, Local Consumer Review Survey 2026 · www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/

AI summaries answer before anyone clicks

Pew Research Center tracked the Google searches of 900 US adults in March 2025 and published the results in July 2025. 18% of searches produced an AI summary. With one on the page, users clicked a regular result on 8% of visits, against 15% without, and clicked a link inside the summary on 1% of visits.

Question searches were the most likely to get a summary: 60% of searches starting with words like who, what, when or why produced one. That is exactly how patients phrase their questions before treatment.

SourcePew Research Center, Google users are less likely to click on links when an AI summary appears in the results, 2025 · www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-ar…

Google's own documentation on AI features says there are no additional requirements or special optimizations needed to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode. It also says clicks from pages with AI Overviews are higher quality, meaning longer visits. That is Google's claim, not an independent measurement.

That answer is invented, but its shape is not. When I test these prompts for clients, assistants repeat checkable facts: rating, review recency, insurance plans by name, hours and online booking. If a fact is not public, the assistant cannot use it, which is how Practice C becomes an afterthought.

Side evidence from a non-dental client

ITCE is a B2B IT training company in Sofia, not a dental practice. Its visits from AI assistants rose from 8 to 77 in 90 days, and those visitors converted at 10.4%, against 10.6% for Google organic visitors (GA4, 19 June to 16 September 2026, against the 90 days before). Small numbers, but not idle browsers.

The dental example I trust most is a blog post for a private fee for service practice in the Maryland/DC area. It answers the question patients ask the week before they commit to an implant: how long healing takes. It did not exist before we started, and it drew 9,971 visits in 15 months, more than the homepage's 6,202.

What this means for your practice: write down the five questions patients ask before they book, and answer each one on a page that names your practice, your dentists and your town. My guide to AI search for dentists shows how to check what the assistants currently say about you.

How do patients build a shortlist from reviews?

They filter first, then they read. BrightLocal found that 97% of consumers read online reviews for local businesses, and 41% always do, up from 29% a year earlier. Tebra's healthcare number is lower but still decisive: 79% of patients read reviews before they choose a provider.

The filters got stricter in 2026. In BrightLocal's survey, 68% will only use a business with four stars or more, up from 55% in 2025, and 31% require 4.5 stars or higher, up from 17%. Tebra found that 53% of patients won't consider a provider with fewer than four stars.

SourceBrightLocal, Local Consumer Review Survey 2026 · www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/

Recency is the filter most practices miss. To a patient, 400 reviews and nothing since spring can look like a practice that stopped seeing people.

Replies get read too. 89% of consumers expect owners to respond to reviews, 80% say they are likely to use a business that responds to all of them, and 50% are put off by generic or templated replies. In Tebra's survey, 70% of patients said a provider's reply to a negative review improved their opinion.

Two review rules that do not bend for a dental practice

Never offer anything in exchange for a review. The FTC final rule on fake reviews and testimonials, announced in August 2024, bans incentives conditioned on a particular sentiment and undisclosed reviews by company insiders. My own rule on top: ask every patient after a normal visit, not only the ones you expect to be happy.

Never confirm in a public reply that the reviewer is a patient, and never mention treatment. Thank them, apologize if it went badly, and give a name and a number to call.

What this means for your practice: run reviews as a weekly flow, not a lifetime total. Two or three new reviews a week means the newest one is never more than a few days old. My guide to Google reviews for dentists has the ask scripts and reply templates.

What do patients check on your website before they call?

They check whether you are real, whether you take their insurance, roughly what it will cost, and how to get in. BrightLocal found that 54% of consumers visit a business's website after reading positive reviews, up from 32% when it last asked in 2019.

Tebra's survey puts a number on the stakes: 44% of patients say a practice's website influences their choice of provider, and 16% would consider switching practices if a provider's website is outdated.

Cost sits behind most of these questions. The Peterson-KFF Health System Tracker, using 2023 National Health Interview Survey data, found that 21% of adults delayed or went without dental care because of cost. The ADA Health Policy Institute report on dental care use, coverage and cost barriers says dental care has the highest cost barriers of any category of health care, and that 24% of Americans had no dental coverage in 2024.

Patients check the dentist, not just the practice. At Kraja & Sidhu Dental in Silver Spring, MD, roughly 600 people a month search the two doctors by name. That traffic used to land on directory profiles the practice could not edit; in year two, the two new bio pages drew 277 and 257 organic views.

What this means for your practice: put four answers (who, insurance, cost, how to book) within one tap of every service page. My guide to service pages that rank without backlinks shows the page structure I use.

Do patients still call, or do they book online?

Both, and a practice that offers only one door loses the people who wanted the other. In Tebra's survey, 41% of patients said they prefer phone calls for scheduling, 34% said online scheduling is important, and 65% prioritize getting an appointment as soon as possible.

Google makes room for both. A Business Profile can carry a booking link, which Google's help page on managing local business links lists as a way for customers to book an appointment. Without one, the patient deciding at 10 pm has to remember to call you tomorrow.

What happens between the booking and the first visit?

The journey does not end at the booking. Tebra found that half of respondents value immediate confirmations and reminders, 48% prefer texts for reminders and follow-ups, and 31% have run into mobile forms that don't work well.

That last number is about intake as much as marketing. A patient who books at night and then gets a PDF to print has learned how the rest of the relationship will feel.

The first visit is also where your next review comes from. What this means for your practice: confirm within minutes, send a text reminder, make every form work on a phone, and ask for a review after a normal visit.

Frequently asked questions

Do patients really use ChatGPT to find a dentist?
Some do, but I could not find a published dental-only figure I trust. BrightLocal's 45% covers all local businesses. Your own data is the better test: in GA4, look for referral visits from chatgpt.com, gemini.google.com and perplexity.ai, and ask the front desk to note when a new patient says an assistant suggested you.
How many Google reviews does a dental practice need?
There is no magic total, but there is a floor and a clock. BrightLocal found that 47% of consumers won't use a business with fewer than 20 reviews, so 20 is the first target. After that, recency beats count: 60 reviews with three from this week usually look healthier to a patient than 400 with nothing since spring.
Should we offer online booking if we prefer patients to call?
Yes, and you decide what is bookable. Many practices open only new patient exams and consultations online, or use request-based booking where the front desk confirms the slot. Keep the phone number just as visible. The aim is to catch the patient who decides at 10 pm, not to replace your front desk.
Should a dental website show prices?
For elective work, I recommend at least a typical range or a "from" price, plus financing options and a note on how insurance applies. An AI assistant cannot repeat a number you never published, and a patient comparing three practices will call the one that gave them something to go on. You do not need a full fee schedule.
How can I tell which step of the journey is losing patients?
Measure each step separately. In Google Business Profile, compare views with website clicks, calls and booking clicks. In GA4, compare form starts with completed forms; at the Maryland practice, 376 people started a form without finishing it, which shows exactly where to look next. Then compare tracked calls with the front desk log. The biggest drop is where to start.
Is the journey different for implant and cosmetic patients?
It is longer and more careful, because the fee is larger. These patients ask more questions before they contact anyone, which is why one post on implant healing time became the Maryland practice's most visited page. As Dr. Randeep Sidhu of Kraja & Sidhu Dental put it: "when someone's about to invest a significant amount, they want to know you're reputable."
