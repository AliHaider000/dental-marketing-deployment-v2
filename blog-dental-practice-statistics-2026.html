Dental Practice Statistics 2026: What the Latest Numbers on Patients, Revenue and Staffing Mean for Your Practice

US dental spending reached $189.2 billion in 2024, yet nearly two in five dentists now say they are not busy enough. Here are the 2026 numbers on patients, revenue and staffing, each traced to its publisher, with what each one means for a private practice.

By Max Lezginov11 min readEvery number traced to its publisherWorksheet included, pen not suppliedPublished 20 October 2026

In the ADA Health Policy Institute's Q3 2026 State of the U.S. Dental Economy report, published in October 2026, only about 26% of dentists said they were confident in the U.S. economy, and nearly two in five said they were not busy enough. That is the backdrop for every budget, hiring and marketing decision a private practice makes this quarter.

Most dental statistics pages recycle undated numbers from aggregator sites. I wanted one I could hand to a practice owner without caveats, so every figure below comes from the publisher's own page or PDF, with its data year next to it. Each section ends with what the number means for one private practice, because an average only helps if it changes a decision.

Quick answer

US dental spending reached $189.2 billion in 2024, but consumer dental spending in August 2026 was 0.9% below a year earlier. There were 205,088 active dentists in 2025, general dentists averaged $215,320 in income, two in three adults had a dental exam or cleaning, and most practices still find hygienists very hard to hire.

Key takeaways
  • Spending: Americans spent $189.2 billion on dental services in 2024, up 6.6% before inflation (CMS National Health Expenditure data).
  • Busyness: nearly two in five dentists said they were not busy enough in Q3 2026, up from around one quarter in Q2 (ADA HPI).
  • Margins: over five years, general dentists' inflation-adjusted revenue rose 1.4% while expenses rose 4.9% (ADA HPI, 2026).
  • Hiring: 88.3% of dentists recruiting hygienists in Q3 2026 called it very or extremely challenging (ADA HPI).
  • Patients: 47% of US consumers will not use a local business with fewer than 20 reviews (BrightLocal, 2026, all industries).
Data checked 20 October 2026

I opened every source on this page on 20 October 2026. Data years lag publication dates, so each figure carries its own: CMS spending is 2024 data, ADA income and workforce figures are 2025, BLS pay is May 2025, CDC visits are 2025, and the ADA confidence and staffing panel is Q3 2026.

At the first mention of each survey I give who ran it, who paid where relevant, the sample and the dates. Vendor surveys are labeled as such.

How big is US dental spending, and is it still growing?

It is large and growing over the long run, but it slowed in 2026. Americans spent $189.2 billion on dental services in 2024, up 6.6% from 2023 before inflation, according to the CMS National Health Expenditure highlights for 2024, released in January 2026. Total US health spending rose 7.2% to $5.3 trillion.

The ADA Health Policy Institute's analysis of the same data puts dental at 3.6% of all health spending, unchanged from 2023. After inflation, dental spending grew 4%, against 4.6% for health care overall. Government programs grew 9%, largely because of Medicare, while private dental insurance grew 2.3% and out-of-pocket spending 3.3%.

SourceCMS, National Health Expenditure Highlights 2024; ADA HPI, National Dental Expenditures · ada.org/resources/research/health-policy-institute/dental-…

The newer signal is weaker. In the HPI's Q3 2026 report, which uses Bureau of Economic Analysis data, consumer dental spending in August 2026 was down 0.9% from a year earlier and down 2.4% year to date. Over ten years it rose 20%, while spending on physician services rose 51%.

SourceADA HPI, State of the U.S. Dental Economy, Q3 2026 (Bureau of Economic Analysis data) · www.ada.org/-/media/project/ada-organization/ada/ada-org/f…
What this means for your practice

The market is not shrinking, but it is not growing fast enough to carry a practice that stands still. This year's growth comes from winning patients in your own ZIP codes, which means being the practice people find and then choose. That is a visibility and front desk problem, not a market problem.

How many dentists are there, and how is the workforce changing?

There were 205,088 professionally active dentists in the US in 2025, or 60.0 per 100,000 people, according to the ADA HPI dentist workforce data. The ratio has barely moved in a decade: it was 61.0 in 2015, per HPI's U.S. Dentist Workforce report (August 2025).

Who the dentists are is changing faster than how many there are. Women made up 39.4% of dentists in 2025, up from 19.9% in 2005. The average retirement age was 68.7 in 2024, up from 64.7 in 2001, and HPI expects most baby boomer dentists to leave the workforce by the late 2020s. Ownership is falling too: 73% of dentists owned their practice in 2023, against 85% in 2005.

SourceADA HPI, Dentist workforce (2025 update) and The U.S. Dentist Workforce, August 2025 · www.ada.org/en/resources/research/health-policy-institute/…
What this means for your practice

Every retirement near you sends patients looking for a new dentist, and most will start that search online. If a practice nearby is selling or closing, your Google Business Profile, reviews and service pages need to be in order before those patients start looking, not after.

How confident are dentists right now?

Less confident than in the first half of the year, and confidence in the US economy is at its lowest in several years. In the HPI's Q3 2026 report, 26.4% of dentists were somewhat or very confident in the US economy, 40.9% in the dental care sector and 50.4% in their own practice. The panel invited 2,422 dentists on 14 September 2026; 750 responded, 704 of them in private practice.

The drop followed two quarters of improvement. Confidence in their own practice had reached 60.6% in Q2 2026, so it fell about ten points in one quarter.

SourceADA HPI, State of the U.S. Dental Economy, Q3 2025 to Q3 2026 reports · www.ada.org/resources/research/health-policy-institute/den…

Busyness tells the same story. Nearly two in five dentists said they were not busy enough in Q3 2026, up from around one quarter in Q2. The average wait for a new patient appointment fell to 12 days, a new low in HPI's series, from 12.4 days in Q3 2025 and 14.3 days in Q3 2024.

What this means for your practice

Nearly two in five dentists now have open chair time, and 12 days is the national wait a new patient is comparing you against. Check how far away your next new patient opening is. If it is weeks out, fix the schedule before spending on marketing. If it is days out and chairs are empty, the problem is being found.

What do dentists earn, and is revenue keeping up with expenses?

Revenue is not keeping up. General dentists' average income was $215,320 in 2025, flat after inflation and lower than 15 years ago, according to the HPI's Trends in dentists' income, revenue and hours worked (June 2026, Survey of Dental Practice data). HPI calls the cause a fiscal squeeze: expenses rising faster than revenue.

The slide deck puts numbers on it, in 2025 dollars. Comparing the 2016 to 2020 average with 2021 to 2025, revenue per general dentist rose 1.4% to $804,144, expenses rose 4.9% to $552,909, and income fell 8.1% to $244,729. Owners took the hit: owner general dentists' incomes fell $23,000 between 2011 to 2015 and 2021 to 2025, while non-owner incomes were essentially unchanged.

For a wider comparison, the Bureau of Labor Statistics puts median pay for all dentists at $176,110 in May 2025. That figure mixes employees and owners, so the HPI numbers are the better benchmark if you own the practice.

SourceADA HPI, State of the U.S. Dental Economy, Q3 2026 · www.ada.org/-/media/project/ada-organization/ada/ada-org/f…
What this means for your practice

Fees have not kept pace with costs, so profit increasingly depends on filling chairs you already pay to staff. In a squeeze, knowing which channel produced last month's new patients matters more, not less, because it tells you which spending to keep.

How hard is it to hire hygienists and assistants?

Still very hard. In the HPI's Q3 2026 panel, around one third of dentists were recruiting hygienists, and 88.3% of them called it very or extremely challenging. About a third were recruiting assistants, and 76.8% said the same. The report's own summary is that staffing shortages have not eased much in the past year.

The BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook shows why. Hygienist jobs are projected to grow 8% from 2025 to 2035, much faster than average, with about 15,000 openings a year. Dental assistant jobs are projected to grow 7%, with about 53,000 openings a year.

SourceU.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Occupational Outlook Handbook, pages modified 27 August 2026 · www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/dental-hygienists.htm

Vacancies stay open a long time. Among dentists who filled a hygienist position, 21.2% said it had been open for more than six months, and only 9.6% filled it in under two weeks.

SourceADA HPI, State of the U.S. Dental Economy, Q3 2026 · www.ada.org/-/media/project/ada-organization/ada/ada-org/f…
What this means for your practice

A hygiene chair you cannot staff loses money, and so does one you staff but cannot fill. Before spending more on new patient marketing, check that you have the hygiene hours to absorb them. Before cutting marketing, check whether empty chairs or empty schedules are the real problem.

How many adults actually see a dentist?

About two in three. 66.7% of US adults had a dental exam or cleaning in the past 12 months in 2025, according to CDC NCHS FastStats, which uses the early-release 2025 National Health Interview Survey estimates. The final 2025 figure in the NHIS adult summary statistics is 66.4%, up from 63.0% in 2020.

SourceCDC NCHS, NHIS Adult Summary Health Statistics · wwwn.cdc.gov/NHISDataQueryTool/SHS_adult/index.html

The Delta Dental Plans Association's 2026 State of America's Oral Health and Wellness Report gives a lower figure: 57% of adults said they visited a dentist in 2025. Delta Dental is a dental insurer; it commissioned Material Holdings to survey 1,000 adults and 1,000 parents through an online opt-in panel from 5 to 15 January 2026, with a margin of error of plus or minus 3%.

Among adults who went, 89% had a preventive visit, up from 84% in 2024. Rural adults lagged: 44% visited, against 59% of urban adults. The gap with the CDC comes from different questions, samples and methods, so use the CDC figure as the national baseline and Delta Dental for attitudes.

What this means for your practice

One adult in three is not seeing anyone, and most who do come for prevention. Your recall system decides how many of your own patients stay in the two thirds. Pull the share of active patients seen in the last 12 months from your practice management software and set it next to 66.7%.

How do patients choose and judge a practice online?

They read reviews and set thresholds before they call. BrightLocal, which sells review and local SEO software, surveyed 1,002 US adults through a SurveyMonkey panel for its Local Consumer Review Survey, published 11 February 2026. It covers all local businesses, not dental. 97% read online reviews, 68% will only use a business rated 4.0 or higher, and 47% will not use one with fewer than 20 reviews.

SourceBrightLocal, Local Consumer Review Survey 2026 · www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/

Healthcare data points the same way. Tebra, which sells practice management and patient engagement software, surveyed 3,964 US adults online on 29 June 2025 for its sixth annual Patient Perspectives report. 79% read reviews before choosing a provider, 53% will not consider one rated under 4 stars, 56% start their research on Google, and 70% said a reply to a negative review improved their opinion.

What this means for your practice

Rating, count and recency are three separate filters, and many practices pass the first two and fail the third. Twenty reviews from 2023 do less work than eight from this quarter. I cover how to get more Google reviews without breaking the rules separately, and how patients choose a dentist in 2026 goes deeper on the decision itself.

How are patients using AI for health questions and local recommendations?

A growing minority use it, and the share depends on what is being asked. Four recent surveys measure different things, so read them side by side rather than as one number.

SourcePew Research Center, KFF, Gallup and BrightLocal (each linked in the text) · www.pewresearch.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/20/2026/06/PI…

Pew Research Center surveyed 5,119 US adults on its American Trends Panel from 17 to 23 February 2026, with a margin of error of plus or minus 1.6 points. About half (49%) use AI chatbots, and 20% use them for medical advice. Use falls with age: 61% of adults aged 18 to 29 use ChatGPT, against 19% of those 65 and older.

KFF found that 32% of adults used AI chatbots for health information in the past year, and 77% worry about the privacy of medical information shared with AI (1,343 adults, 24 February to 2 March 2026, plus or minus 3 points). The West Health-Gallup Center on Healthcare in America, a partnership with West Health, a nonprofit funded by philanthropists Gary and Mary West, found that 25% of adults have used AI for health information or advice. 14% of recent users said it led them to skip a visit (5,660 adults, 27 October to 22 December 2025, plus or minus 2.1 points).

Side evidence from outside dentistry, from my own client work: for ITCE, a B2B IT training company in Sofia, visits from AI assistants rose from 8 to 77 in 90 days, and those visitors converted at 10.4% against 10.6% for Google organic visitors (GA4, 19 June to 16 September 2026 against the previous 90 days). The numbers are small, but the AI visitors were not lower quality.

What this means for your practice

In BrightLocal's 2026 survey, ChatGPT and other AI tools became the third most popular source of business recommendations, behind Google and Facebook. Their answers draw on your website, your profile and your reviews. The guide to AI search for dentists covers what you can influence.

Why do your own numbers matter more than any national average?

Because none of the figures above tells you whether your marketing works. I have not found a primary-source national benchmark for new patients per month; the "20 to 40 a month" figures that circulate trace back to agency and vendor blogs. The only number you can trust is the one counted in your own accounts.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a national benchmark for new patients per month?
Not one I would cite. I have not found a primary-source benchmark for new patients per month per practice, and the figures that circulate trace back to agency and vendor blogs. Practice size, location, fee model and insurance mix move the answer so much that one average would mislead you anyway. Count your own tracked calls, forms and booking requests for 90 days and use that as your baseline.
Do these national numbers apply to my state or city?
Only roughly. HPI notes that the number of dentists per 100,000 people varies by state, so your local competition can look very different from the national 60.0. For staff pay, use the BLS wage data for your metro area, linked from each Occupational Outlook Handbook page, rather than the national median. The patient surveys are national samples, so treat them as direction, not local fact.
Are vendor surveys like BrightLocal and Tebra worth using?
Yes, with care. Both publish their sample and method, which many statistics pages do not. Both also sell software related to what they measure, and neither is dental only: BrightLocal covers all local businesses, Tebra all healthcare. I use them for direction and size of change, such as the share requiring 4.5 stars rising from 17% to 31% in a year, not as precise dental benchmarks.
Which of these numbers should I check first?
Your new patient wait time and your tracked inquiries. Wait time tells you whether you have room for more patients; the HPI national average is 12 days. Tracked inquiries tell you whether your marketing is producing them. If both look healthy and chairs are still empty, the leak sits between the phone and the schedule, which is a front desk conversation rather than a marketing one.
Where do the ADA figures come from?
The confidence, busyness and staffing figures come from the HPI's quarterly panel of dentists who actively treat patients; Q3 2026 had a 31.2% adjusted response rate. Income, revenue and hours come from the annual Survey of Dental Practice, which HPI weights for oversampling and nonresponse and reports in 2025 dollars. Workforce counts come from HPI's dentist supply data, published as a spreadsheet covering 2001 to 2025.
How often will this page be updated?
When a primary source publishes a new edition. The HPI dental economy report is quarterly, so confidence, busyness, wait times and staffing change four times a year. Income, workforce and CMS spending data change about once a year. Each update will carry a dated note saying what changed, and the date will not move unless the numbers did.
